Install fx-mic-mod

Back up the samples and settings on your mic (the EP-2350, sold as the Ting and as the FX Mic), swap Teenage Engineering's firmware for CircuitPython, install fx-mic-mod in one click, and get back to stock whenever you want. Each step runs in the browser while the mic is plugged in, with a drag-and-drop route if the browser won't allow it. You only do this once. After that, you change presets in the preset editor.

Drive and serial access are blocked in this view. Chrome and Edge only let a page touch USB drives and serial ports when it runs as its own tab, and this page is running inside a frame. Every step still works by drag and drop. For one-click flashing, save the standalone copy and open it in Chrome or Edge on your computer.

Before you start

  • A desktop computer with Chrome or Edge for the one-click tools. Safari and Firefox can still follow the drag-and-drop route.
  • A USB-C cable that carries data. Many charge-only cables power the mic but never show a drive.
  • Take off the lower lid to reach the USB-C port and the small reset button above it.
The mic has no charging circuit, so USB only powers it and won't charge AA batteries. On USB it stays off until you squeeze the handle all the way in.
01

Back up the mic's disk

Flashing CircuitPython erases everything on the device's own drive: your samples 1.wav to 4.wav, config.json, and any other files. Copy them off first.

  1. Plug in the mic and squeeze the handle to power it on.
  2. A drive called TINGDISK appears. Some firmware versions and the official manual call it FX-MIC DISK.
  3. Pick that drive below. The page reads every file and saves them as one zip.

Doing it by hand

Open the drive in Finder or File Explorer and copy every file into a folder on your computer named something like ting-backup-2026-10-22. Include hidden files if you have edited config files with a text editor.

02

Download everything first

From step 03 on you'll be holding the handle in, so get all three downloads now and keep them in one folder, for example fx-mic-mod in Downloads.

  1. CircuitPython 10.3.1 for the EP-2350, the same file for the Ting and the FX Mic: adafruit-circuitpython-teenage_engineering_ep2350-en_US-10.3.1.uf2. Other versions and languages are on circuitpython.org.
  2. Adafruit's 10.x library bundle from circuitpython.org/libraries. Pick the 10.x one, not the newest listed: its files only load on the CircuitPython version they were built for. Unzip it. Step 05 needs its lib folder.
  3. The stock firmware, your way back to the original mic. Pick the latest version below and unzip it, so you have its .uf2.

Optional: full flash dump with picotool

If you want a byte-for-byte image of the chip (firmware plus the disk), Raspberry Pi's picotool can read the RP2350's flash while the mic is in boot mode (step 03):

picotool info -a
picotool save -a ting-full-flash.bin

UnverifiedNobody has published a picotool dump of an EP-2350 yet. TE changed the boot-mode USB IDs in firmware 1.0.5, so picotool may not recognise the device, and the chip could have reads locked. The official .uf2 above is the dependable restore path.

03

Put the mic in boot mode

Boot mode is how new firmware gets onto the mic. First plug the mic into your computer with a USB-C data cable, and leave it plugged in until you've finished step 04. It doesn't matter whether the mic is on or off: the reset button restarts it either way, so there's no need to turn it off first.

1 · Squeeze Hold the handle all the way in, and keep holding it through the end of section 04, after the flash finishes and the mic reboots. Grab a rubber band if it's helpful.
2 · Double-click reset The small dark button just above the USB-C port, under the lower lid. The timing is fussy, so try a few times.
3 · Look for the drive All the lights go out. That's normal: the mic is waiting for new firmware, not off. Within a few seconds a new drive appears on your computer holding INDEX.HTM and INFO_UF2.TXT. It's called RP2350 once CircuitPython has been installed, and TING BOOT or FX MIC BOOT while the stock firmware is on. If the lights stay on and no new drive appears, the double-click was too slow: try again.
Keep the handle squeezed until the boot drive disappears and CIRCUITPY shows up. In boot mode nothing else keeps the mic powered, so letting go mid-copy cuts power to the chip. Once CircuitPython is running it holds the power on itself, and you can let go.
04

Flash CircuitPython

With the mic in boot mode and the handle still squeezed, choose the CircuitPython .uf2 you downloaded in step 02, then pick the boot drive.

The drive disappears as soon as the copy finishes, the mic restarts, and a new drive named CIRCUITPY mounts. Your computer may complain that the drive was removed without ejecting. That's expected.

      
Doing it by hand
Drag the .uf2 file onto the boot drive (RP2350, TING BOOT or FX MIC BOOT) in Finder or File Explorer, keep squeezing, and wait for CIRCUITPY to appear.

      
Now you can let go of the handle. Once CIRCUITPY shows up, CircuitPython is running and keeps the mic powered on its own.

    


    
    

      
05
Install fx-mic-mod

      
This copies the fx-mic-mod files to CIRCUITPY: code.py and its modules (autotune.py, recorder.py, config_loader.py), boot.py, and a starter config.json with four presets. The copies on the mic leave out the source comments, which saves about 50 KB for recordings. The commented source is in the project repository.

      
It also needs Adafruit's driver for the mic's audio chip, adafruit_nau88l21.mpy. Choose the lib folder from the 10.x library bundle you unzipped in step 02. Only the driver and the adafruit_register folder it needs are copied from it. You can skip this if both are already on the drive.

After installing, turn the mic off and on again. To turn it off, hold the orange and white buttons together until the lights go out (about a second), then let go. If it stays on, unplug the USB cable and take out the batteries. Once it's dark, squeeze the handle to turn it back on. Working looks like: white LED 1 stays lit, the red LEDs are off (the clean spot), and CIRCUITPY shows up on your computer again. If white LED 1 instead blinks twice every few seconds, the code stopped on an error: connect the serial console below and click Reload to see it. From then on, a normal power-on (just squeeze the handle) lets the mic save recordings, and your computer sees the drive as read-only. To save presets from the preset editor or copy files, hold the orange button while you squeeze the handle to power on. That's edit mode. You also need edit mode to run this install again later.

      
Optional: copy samples from your backup
        

          
If your step 01 backup has WAV files you want on the mic, this copies them to CIRCUITPY. fx-mic-mod plays 16-bit mono WAVs at 24 kHz, so each file is converted to that on the way. Bought or downloaded samples are usually much louder than a voice through the mic, so by default they're also turned down to about the level the mic records at. Choose the backup zip or the WAV files, then pick CIRCUITPY with the mic in edit mode. A file whose name starts with 1 to 4 goes in that slot; any others fill the empty slots in order.

        Hardware smoke test (code.py)
        

          
If something seems wrong, this small test checks the buttons, LEDs, handle and volume knob on their own. Squeezing the handle fills the white LEDs from the bottom, the top, middle and bottom buttons light red LEDs 1 to 3, and red LED 4 comes on past half volume. Every change prints to the serial console. Save it over code.py to run it, and reinstall fx-mic-mod afterwards.

          

          

        

      

    


    
    

      
06
Watch it run on the serial console

      
The mic reports what it's doing over the USB cable: which preset is on, how far the handle is squeezed, and any error that stops it. This is where you look when something doesn't work, and where to copy text from if you're asking for help.

      
    
        
  1. With the mic on and plugged in, click Connect. Chrome or Edge asks which device to use: pick the one named CircuitPython or USB Serial, then Connect.
    2. 
        
  2. Click Reload. The mic restarts its code, and you should see code.py output: followed by lines like TOP button = next preset (5 spots, 0 = clean). That means it's running.
    3. 
        
  3. Use the mic. Each press and squeeze prints a line: preset 2 of 4, paddle pressed, volume +3.0 dB. An overflow line means that preset is too heavy for the chip and the sound will glitch.
    4. 
        
  4. If you see Traceback, the code stopped on an error and the mic goes quiet, with white LED 1 blinking twice every few seconds. Copy everything from Traceback down: the last line says what went wrong.
    5. 
      

      
Stop halts the code and gives you a >>> prompt, where you can type Python into the box below and click Send, for example when someone helping you asks you to run a line. Reload starts the code again. With the cursor in the box, control-C and control-D do the same as the two buttons (on a Mac that's control, not ⌘).

Lining the log up with an audio recording
Tick Timestamps and each line gets the time since the clock started, like [01:23.4]. Start your audio recording, then click Mark and squeeze the handle right away: the paddle pressed line and the moment your voice starts in the recording are the same instant, so every later line can be found in the audio by its time. Save log downloads the console as a text file.

      
Without a browser serial port
Use the Mu editor's Serial button, or screen /dev/tty.usbmodem* 115200 on macOS, or PuTTY on Windows with the COM port from Device Manager.

    


    
    

      
07
Go back to stock

      
This puts Teenage Engineering's own firmware back and copies your backed-up samples onto it. You need the stock firmware from step 02 and your backup zip from step 01.

      
    
        
  1. Plug the mic into your computer with a USB-C data cable. Leave it plugged in until the end.
    2. 
        
  2. Have the stock .uf2 ready before you start: the one you unzipped in step 02. If you don't have it, download and unzip it now.
    3. 
        
  3. Put the mic in boot mode. The mic can be on or off. Squeeze the handle all the way in and keep holding it until step 4 finishes. Under the lower lid, double-click the small dark button just above the USB-C port.
    Worked: all the lights go out, and a drive called RP2350 appears on your computer (CIRCUITPY goes away). The lights going out is normal.
    Didn't work: the lights stay on and CIRCUITPY is still there. Double-click again, a bit faster. If it still won't go, keep squeezing and use Reboot to boot mode in the step 06 console.
    4. 
        
  4. Flash the stock firmware. In step 04, click Choose the .uf2 file and pick TE's file, then Pick the boot drive and flash and choose RP2350.
    Worked: RP2350 disappears within a few seconds, the mic restarts, a white LED lights, and a drive called TINGDISK or FX-MIC DISK appears. Now you can let go of the handle. Your computer may warn that the drive wasn't ejected, and the page may say the drive went away during the write. Both are expected: what counts is the new drive appearing.
    5. 
        
  5. Copy your samples back with the tool below: choose your unzipped backup folder, then pick the TINGDISK or FX-MIC DISK drive. Or copy the files across by hand.
    6. 
        
  6. Check it: squeeze and sing. You should hear the stock presets again.
    7.
